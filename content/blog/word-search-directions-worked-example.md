A word can look hidden even when you are looking straight at it. Often the letters are simply running in a direction you have not tried yet. Instead of scanning the whole board faster, choose one starting letter and check its neighbours slowly.

These two examples use actual fixed boards from Puzzlepia's [Daily Word Search](/daily/word-search/). You can read the examples here, leave the answers closed, and then open the same practice set to try the selection yourself. There is no timer and no need to finish every word in one visit.

## Start with a direction, not a guess

In this game, each word follows a straight line: across, down, or diagonally, in either direction. Letters cannot turn a corner halfway through a word. When you find a possible first letter, look for the second letter in a neighbouring cell. That pair gives you a direction to test.

Keep moving by the same amount for each next letter. For a horizontal word, stay on the same row. For a diagonal word, move one row and one column at each step. If the next letter is wrong, leave that route and try another neighbour or starting point.

## Try a backward word: KIWI

Here is the Fruit bowl practice board. Rows run from top to bottom, and columns run from left to right. Find **KIWI** before opening the answer. You need four neighbouring letters in one straight line.

Look along row 6, starting at its right-hand end. Reading backward means moving left through the grid, not changing the spelling of KIWI.
{{< /puzzle-reveal >}}

{{< puzzle-reveal title="Show the KIWI path" >}}
Start at **row 6, column 6**. Move left through columns **6, 5, 4, 3**: K, I, W, I. The row stays the same throughout. In the game, tap the K and then the final I, or drag between those endpoints.
{{< /puzzle-reveal >}}

Repeated letters can be useful checkpoints. KIWI contains two I letters, but they do not sit beside each other in the word. After K and I, check for W before accepting the final I. Recognising familiar letters is only the start; their order still matters.

## Try a diagonal word: STAR

The Night Sky board uses the same rules. This time, find **STAR** without assuming that the letters share a row or column.

{{< puzzle-reveal title="Hint for STAR" >}}
Start with the S in the top-left corner. Look one row down and one column to the right for the next letter.
{{< /puzzle-reveal >}}

{{< puzzle-reveal title="Show the STAR path" >}}
The path is **row 1, column 1** (S), **row 2, column 2** (T), **row 3, column 3** (A), and **row 4, column 4** (R). Each step moves down and right by one cell. Select the first S and the final R on the playable board.
{{< /puzzle-reveal >}}

A diagonal can feel less obvious because rows make strong visual tracks. Try following only one diagonal with your eyes instead of jumping between nearby letters. If you reach a board edge before finishing the word, that route cannot be the answer.

[Play the Night Sky set](/daily/word-search/?set=night-sky), or save the [Trace STAR Diagonally challenge](/daily/challenges/star-diagonal-word-search/) for another break.

## Take a small hint when you need one

On the playable board, **Show a starting letter** outlines the first letter of an unfound word and names its row and column. It does not finish the word or increase your progress. You still choose the direction and endpoint. Press it again to see a starting letter for another unfound word.

The word-list buttons are a different kind of help: they reveal the whole word and mark it found. Use either option when it suits your break. Reset clears found words and hints so you can try the same board again.

## Make the next break a different exercise

You do not need a faster time to make a replay worthwhile. After practising the backward route, try the diagonal example without opening its answer. On another visit, choose a different practice set and check first letters systematically.

These are curated, repeatable sets, not an endless supply of new boards. The [Daily Puzzles page](/daily/) also offers block, ring, number, and matching puzzles when you want a different kind of short session. Start with one clear goal, and stop whenever your break is over.
