## Four reveals, one next choice

Imagine these first two turns on Memory Pairs Layout 3:

1. Open **card 1: moon**, then **card 2: diamond**. They do not match.
2. Open **card 3: circle**, then **card 4: star**. They do not match either.

On the next turn, **card 7 reveals a diamond**. Which card should you choose to complete that pair? After finding it, you open **card 8: moon**. Which earlier location helps now?

Keep a symbol and its position together. A mismatch from an earlier turn is still useful information, even after those cards turn face down. Position numbers identify places; you match symbols, not numbers.

This is a fixed, untimed read-and-solve exercise using the game's twelve-card Layout 3. The whole board is optional; the reveals above are enough to answer both questions.

{{< puzzle-reveal title="Show a small hint" >}}
The two symbols in the first turn appear again at cards 7 and 8. Remember which was at 1 and which was at 2. The circle and star in the second turn belong to different pairs.
{{< /puzzle-reveal >}}

{{< puzzle-reveal title="Show the choices and explanation" >}}
After **card 7: diamond**, choose **card 2**. Both show diamonds. Card 1 was a moon, so choosing it would repeat a mismatch rather than use the known partner.

After **card 8: moon**, choose **card 1**. The first turn has now helped you find two pairs: **7 with 2**, then **8 with 1**. The more recent circle and star do not replace those earlier facts.

For a complete practice route, the remaining pairs are:

- **12 and 3**: circles.
- **11 and 4**: stars.
- **9 and 6**: triangles.
- **10 and 5**: suns.

Every position appears in exactly one pair. There is no need to memorise this complete route to enjoy the game. Remembering just one useful location is enough for a next move.
