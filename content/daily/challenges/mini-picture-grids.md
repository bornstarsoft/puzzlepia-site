Draw a 5x5 grid on paper or follow the squares in your head. Decide which squares to fill and which to leave empty. The clues on the left describe each row from left to right. The clues above the grid describe each column from top to bottom.

A clue of **3** means exactly three filled squares in a row, with no gap between them. A clue of **2, 1** means a pair followed by a single filled square, with at least one empty square between the two groups. The rest of that line stays empty. Every row and column must match its clues.

## Round 1: Start with a full line

Which row and which column can you fill without guessing? Start there and use those squares to narrow down the others.

{{< picture-grid id="full-line" >}}

{{< puzzle-reveal title="Show the Round 1 hint" >}}
A clue of 5 fills a whole line. Once column C is full, every row with a clue of 1 already has all it needs.
{{< /puzzle-reveal >}}

{{< picture-solution id="full-line" title="Show the Round 1 solution" >}}
Fill all of **row 3 and column C**. Rows 1, 4 and 5 each need just one filled square, so they are already complete. Columns A and E also need just one, which they have in row 3. That leaves **B2 and D2** to finish the run of three in row 2.

The finished shape is a little kite with a tail.

{{< /picture-solution >}}

## Round 2: Make room for a gap

On this board, row 2 needs two groups of two. How can four filled squares and a gap fit into five positions?

{{< picture-grid id="split-groups" >}}

{{< puzzle-reveal title="Show the Round 2 hint" >}}
Two groups of two plus one empty square use the whole row. Once you know what sits in the middle of row 2, use the clue at the top of column C.
{{< /puzzle-reveal >}}

{{< picture-solution id="split-groups" title="Show the Round 2 solution" >}}
Row 2 must be **filled, filled, empty, filled, filled**, and row 3 is full. Columns A and E each have both of their filled squares now, so the other three rows are empty at the edges.

Column C needs a run of three that includes row 3 but cannot use row 2. Fill **C3, C4 and C5**. Row 5 is now complete. Columns B and D each need four filled squares, so fill rows 1 to 4 in those columns. The finished shape is a heart.

{{< /picture-solution >}}
