Two boards, two ways to make a pair. Start with [remembering symbols](#remember-two-symbols) or [making ten](#make-ten-twice). Each is a complete short exercise on its own.

## Remember two symbols

On Memory Pairs Layout 5, card **5** is a Triangle and card **6** is a Diamond. Open the board, find their partners, then close it. Can you name the partner for each card without looking again?

{{< memory-pairs-example layout="5" >}}

{{< puzzle-reveal title="Memory hint" >}}
Both partners are in the bottom row. The Diamond comes before the Triangle there, the reverse of cards 5 and 6.
{{< /puzzle-reveal >}}

{{< puzzle-reveal title="Memory answer" >}}
The **Triangle at 5 matches 10**, and the **Diamond at 6 matches 9**. The symbols are the same within each pair; the card numbers just tell you where they are. A useful way to remember the order is to say "9 Diamond, 10 Triangle" before closing the board.
{{< /puzzle-reveal >}}

[Play Memory Pairs: Layout 5](/daily/memory-pairs/?layout=5). All twelve cards start face down. Try those two pairs first, then find the other four to finish.

## Make ten twice

On Make 10 Layout 5, use only positions **1, 4, 5 and 8** for this question. Divide them into two pairs so that each pair totals **10**. Use each of the four positions once. The other tiles are not part of this question.

{{< number-pairs-example layout="5" >}}

{{< puzzle-reveal title="Make 10 hint" >}}
Position 1 holds a 7. Subtract 7 from 10, then look for that value among the other three positions.
{{< /puzzle-reveal >}}

{{< puzzle-reveal title="Make 10 answer" >}}
Pair **positions 1 and 4** (7 + 3), then **positions 5 and 8** (8 + 2). Unlike Memory Pairs, these two values do not have to be identical. Add the large numbers on the tiles, not their position labels.
{{< /puzzle-reveal >}}

[Play Make 10: Layout 5](/daily/number-pairs/?layout=5). The game starts with all sixteen tiles. Play the two pairs above, then find the six remaining pairs. Partners need not touch.
