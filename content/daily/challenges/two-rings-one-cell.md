## Do the rings need separate cells?

Your tray has a **small pink ring** and a **medium mint ring**. Place both using cells A, B, and C below. Each cell holds at most one ring of each size: small, medium, and large. Existing rings stay fixed. There are **no matches, clears, scores, or color requirements** in this exercise.

{{< ring-practice-board id="shared-cell" >}}

Solid rings show occupied sizes; dashed rings show empty sizes. The labels below each cell give the same information in words. This is a read-and-solve example, not a playable board.

Compare these choices for the medium mint ring:

- Put it in **A**, then find a home for small pink.
- Put it in **B**, then find a home for small pink.

Does one choice prevent you from finishing, or can both work? Try naming the size position each new ring would use.

{{< puzzle-reveal title="Show a small hint" >}}
Small pink needs an empty small position. Only A has one. Would a medium ring in A use that same position, or a different one?
{{< /puzzle-reveal >}}

{{< puzzle-reveal title="Show the placements and explanation" >}}
**Small pink must go in A.** B already has a small mint ring, and C already has a small gold ring. Their colors do not change the occupied-size rule.

**Medium mint can go in A or B.** Both medium positions are empty. C already has a medium gold ring, so it is not compatible.

There are exactly two completed assignments:

1. Small pink in **A**, medium mint in **A**.
2. Small pink in **A**, medium mint in **B**.

In the first assignment, A holds small pink, medium mint, and its original large purple ring. Those are three different sizes. Putting medium mint there first does not take the small position away. Either tray order works for either assignment.

Splitting the new rings across A and B also works. In this placement-only exercise, neither finish earns a higher score or a better result. The goal is simply to place both rings legally.
